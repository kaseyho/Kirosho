# Design brief: ShadeShift

Generated by following the installed Devpost Learn `plan-lens` workflow on 21 October 2026.
The consequential decisions were already answered in the user-supplied
[implementation plan](../docs/implementation_plan.md), which the user instructed
Codex to execute. This brief carries that approved scope forward. It does not claim
an additional interview, field study, or user testing. New implementation choices
are marked **(default)**.

## The one spatial moment

A facilities planner drags one of two shelters across a courtyard model, watches
its useful shade move through the afternoon, and compares an occupancy-aware
placement suggestion before applying it.

Product promise: “Place the shelters you already own where they provide the most
useful shade throughout your activity schedule.”

## Why it belongs on glasses

- Uses: draws in the world and leaves the planner's eyes on the spatial task.
- The planner can inspect the same layout as a compact maquette and at real scale,
  with coverage attached to the activity area it describes. This makes direction,
  shelter spacing, and changing shade directly inspectable. It is a spatial planning
  tool; the prototype does not claim a measured advantage over existing desktop tools.

## Product or overlay

- Type: product; the virtual courtyard model is the experience.
- What the real version would sense: nothing required for v1. The planner supplies
  the site geometry, location, date, activity zones, and occupancy schedule.
- Stand-in for the preview: no recognition stand-in is needed. The courtyard is
  explicitly fictional, and its schedule is illustrative scenario data. The Lens
  uses calculated solar samples, rather than recognizing a real site.

## Where things live

- The planner looks slightly down at the courtyard during the main task.
- Courtyard: 60 × 40 cm at 1:20 scale, representing 12 × 8 m. It remains world-anchored
  for the current session. Start its center about 110 cm forward and 20 cm below
  the initial view, with the table plane tilted toward inspection only if needed
  for visibility **(default, tune against the actual SPECS preview)**.
- Shelters: two north-aligned 3 × 3 m roofs at 2.8 m height. Their visible maquette
  footprint is 15 × 15 cm, with large pinch-and-drag handles.
- Controls: a compact timeline and main action beside the maquette, with a selected
  zone's details next to that zone. Keep supporting controls within the visible
  task area and at least 4 cm across; aim for 6 cm **(default)**.
- Comparison: appears beside the model only when a suggestion or changed layout
  needs review. Include each zone's gain or loss, never only the total.
- Full-scale inspection: renders the same courtyard geometry around predefined
  observation points, with a visible return control. No physical movement is required.

## Hands and input

- Inspect time: pinch-and-drag the timeline; show Singapore time and activity labels.
- Select a zone: pinch a large zone target to inspect its schedule and coverage.
- Move a shelter: pinch-and-drag its handle on the planning plane. Snap to the 0.5 m
  grid on release **(default)**. Show the invalid ghost and reason while invalid;
  release rolls it back to its last valid layout.
- Find better placement: pinch a UI Kit button; show real progress and cancellation.
- Review: preview the suggested geometry and per-zone tradeoffs before Apply.
- Recover: Undo is one action for an applied suggestion; Reset restores the scenario.
- Inspect and commit: use large UI Kit buttons to enter/leave full scale and save a
  reproducible session plan summary.
- Targets are big and highlight when aimed at.
- Voice: none; every operation is available through standard SIK interaction.

## When things show

- Courtyard, time, and a short current result stay visible during planning.
- Zone details show only after zone selection; changing selection replaces them.
- Constraint feedback shows during invalid dragging and clears after rollback.
- Calculation pending preserves the previous result with an Updating indicator.
- Search progress replaces the main action while the search runs; cancellation
  preserves the layout. Editing an input cancels or invalidates the old search.
- A suggestion shows its gains and losses until applied, dismissed, or made stale.
- A committed summary appears after Commit with the scenario and model assumptions.
- Full-scale inspection uses the same state. Return and Recall remain easy to find.
- If a control falls outside the current view: Recall repositions the overview;
  add a small direction cue only if visual testing proves it necessary **(default)**.

## Done criteria

Each one can be checked in Lens Studio's preview.

1. Load the courtyard, move from noon to late afternoon, and select each activity
   zone. The scene visibly updates the roof-shadow geometry and the matching
   standing/seated analysis height; the UI shows the correct time and coverage.
2. Drag either shelter to a valid position and observe recalculation. Drag into
   the boundary, corridor, or the other shelter; the Lens explains the invalid
   position and restores the previous valid layout on release.
3. Run placement search, inspect total and per-zone changes, Apply, then Undo.
   Also verify Cancel, no-better/no-feasible states, and stale-result rejection
   after an edit. Both comparisons use identical solar and schedule inputs.
4. Enter full-scale inspection at a defined point, switch points, and Return or
   Recall the maquette without changing the layout or calculated results.
5. Commit the plan and inspect its reproducible summary (scenario, layout, model
   version and input hashes). Reset works; zero occupancy reports no activity;
   invalid scenario data has a recoverable error. LEAF and pure-engine tests
   verify the relevant states without relying on a narrator.

## Out of scope

- Live camera recognition, site scanning, navigation, persistent spatial anchors,
  multiplayer, voice, a backend, and paid runtime APIs.
- Structural or construction design, wind loads, weather forecasting, and thermal
  simulation.
- Measured cooling, health outcomes, and avoided-carbon claims.
- A continuous global optimum: search reports the best evaluated feasible grid layout.

## Risks and open questions

- Logic and visible preview behavior can be proven; comfort over time and real
  hand-tracking performance remain **feel unproven** without glasses.
- Flat rectangular roofs, a flat site, and uniform people distribution within
  each zone are analytical assumptions. No tree/building shade or weather is modeled.
- The standing (1.6 m) and seated (1.2 m) analysis planes approximate activity heights.
  Shade is not a direct measurement of thermal comfort.
- Use percentage points for coverage changes. Never double-count overlapping shade.
- The one-minute reference must agree with five-minute evaluation within 2 percentage
  points; otherwise use the finer evaluation interval.
- The full scale courtyard exceeds the field of view by design. Local context and a
  clear return control must make predefined-point inspection understandable.
- Overall benefit may reduce one zone's coverage; expose that tradeoff before Apply.
- Occupancy numbers and exact zone/corridor coordinates will be documented as
  illustrative implementation defaults when the scenario fixture is frozen.

## Build notes for CLAD
- Build for SPECS so it runs and can be demonstrated in Lens Studio's preview. No SPECS device is available.
- Use Spectacles Interaction Kit (SIK) and UI Kit for all interactions, so they work with the mouse in Interactive Preview and with LEAF.
- Build any stand-in exactly as described above, and keep it obvious in the scene.
- Use real-world scale in centimeters. Keep content inside the user's view during the main task.
- The display is additive: black renders as transparent. Don't rely on black or very dark content to be seen; keep text light and readable from where the user stands.
- Get the basic flow working first, then add detail.
- Write LEAF scenarios for each done criterion, run them in preview, and report which pass. Say which criteria have no test.
- Don't record, identify, or follow people.

## After the build: judge it
This part is for you, after CLAD finishes.

1. **Does it work?** Ask your agent which LEAF tests passed and which done criteria have no test.
2. **Walk around it.** Open the preview in Interactive Preview. Use WASD to move, Q and E to go down and up, and right-drag (or hold Shift) to look around. Clicking acts as a pinch. Stand where a user would and look at the task.
3. **Ask the three questions.**
   - Does it use what I'm looking at?
   - Does it put something in my space, where it belongs?
   - Does it free my hands or let me keep my eyes up?
   - Would it be just as good on a phone? If so, what would make it need the glasses?
4. **Check comfort.**
   - Everything I need is in view while I do the task. Nothing sits at the edges, behind me, or stuck to my face.
   - Every control is within reach of someone standing there.
   - Text reads easily from where I stand. No dark UI disappearing.
   - Things show when needed and get out of the way.
5. **Label what you know.** Write "logic proven" next to anything the tests and preview show working, and "feel unproven" next to anything only real glasses could tell you (comfort over time, real hand tracking, real recognition).
6. **Fix with design notes, not code.** Tell your agent what to change in design terms, for example: "Move the step card 15 cm lower and next to the pan. Make the Next button twice as big. Re-run the tests." If a fix takes under a minute, like nudging a panel, do it yourself in the scene.
7. **Submit.** Record a video under 3 minutes of your Lens in the preview, public on YouTube. Make your repo public with your Lens Studio project and this `devpost/` folder. Details are on the hackathon site: https://learn-specs.devpost.com/resources
